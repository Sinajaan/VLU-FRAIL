Clinical infection is recorded as an adverse prognostic indicator. Without a suitable adjusted 24-week coefficient, no numeric infection adjustment is applied. The relative ratio is an unadjusted cross-study transport assumption; it may double-count wound severity. It is NOT a validated individualized probability, an adjusted Hart coefficient, or a new measured patient outcome. No fabricated intermediate group is used. No patient-level clinical validity is claimed.

Research prototype only: The baseline is a published ulcer-stratum healing frequency; the adjusted number applies the observed 38/67 frail/non-frail healing ratio from Hart as a hypothetical relative-risk transfer. This is mathematically explicit but unvalidated because the source cohorts differ and the ratio was not adjusted for ulcer characteristics. Never treat the adjusted number as a clinically calibrated patient-specific probability.

Scientific method and references

Healing: Jull et al., J Wound Care 2023, n=802, published Margolis strata 0/1/2: 77.5%, 54.5%, 20.3% complete healing at 24 weeks. Only wound area and duration determine this published group rate; this is not a new multivariable VLU-FRAIL prediction equation. Within-stratum changes do not alter absolute risk; changing the figure smoothly would require a fitted and calibrated model. Week-2 PAR below 25%: Parker et al. 2016 reported adjusted OR 10.07 for 24-week failure; it cannot be added as a probability change. The week-4 40% PAR threshold is exploratory.

Recurrence: Finlayson et al. 2018, prospective validation: 12-month group-observed risk 15.2% low, 60.9% moderate, 66.7% high. These categories refer to published validation-study groups, which must not be assumed equivalent to the live official RECUR website categories. Original tool is linked; protected weighting is not reproduced. The group estimates are conditional on healed ulcer and do not give individual probabilities. Finlayson et al. 2015 cohort observations 22/39/57% at 3/6/12 months are not validated personal predictions.

Sources: Jull 2023 · Parker 2016 · Finlayson 2018 · Hart 2026.
